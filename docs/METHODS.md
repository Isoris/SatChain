# SatChain, Methods paragraph (experimental)

**Satellite DNA (SatChain).** Satellite arrays were annotated with SatChain, a copy-chain framework assembled from established components. Candidate tandem repeats were detected genome-wide without prior annotation. In sliding windows (2 kb, step 1 kb, lags 7–1,000 bp; 10 kb, step 5 kb, lags 1–5 kb), the next-recurrence distances of canonical 10-mers were compared with those of a base-shuffled copy of the same window [NTRprism-like]. Windows exceeding the 99.9th percentile of windows outside known tandem repeats were merged when their dominant lags agreed (±10%). A candidate was retained when ≥3 consecutive copies were supported, i.e. 10-mer recurrence at p, p+L and p+2L above a dinucleotide-block shuffle, or, for units <40 bp, a TRF call [v4.10.0rc2] with ≥3 copies. Candidates were decomposed into monomer copies with StringDecomposer [v1.1.2] using iterative class libraries, in which classes were merged unless ≥5 near-fixed k-mers (k = 5–20; ≥90% vs ≤10% of copies) separated them. Arrays were then defined on the ordered chain of copies by a two-state hidden Markov model (array vs background), whose emissions were confident (complete, ≥80% identity), weak (65–80%) and junk (<65%) copies and gaps, and whose parameters were learned per haplotype by Baum–Welch on 400 arrays. Runs were the maximal stretches with posterior ≥0.95 holding ≥4 confident copies, and each was kept only if its 1-kb tandem signal exceeded the 90th percentile of non-run sequence within candidate intervals. Within runs, windows of max(1 kb, 20 units) carried composition, 4-mer, periodicity, copy-label and local-topology features. Change points were called per feature block by binary segmentation, which at penalties 1.5, 3 and 6 gave nested levels, and were accepted when ≥2 blocks agreed, at least one of them sequence-only. Runs and segments were summarised as paths of states, namely ranked variants, copies tagged by intrinsic annotations, and annotation and insertion nodes. For each path we derived transition matrices, memory and a structure index against shuffled paths, core and accessory edges, and a topology library, which was built by average-linkage clustering on Jensen–Shannon divergence of transitions and composition. Lineage was assigned per copy by k-means clustering of canonical k-mer profiles (k = 5–7, K = 2–6), without using labels as input. Families were defined by consensus identity (≥80% over ≥50%), and tandem gene arrays by an observed gene coverage of ≥0.7 of that expected for one gene per unit. Family tiers were defined by cumulative share of satellite sequence. Every threshold was swept and is reported as a range. Largest satellite blocks are reported as putative centromeric arrays.

Components: NTRprism-style k-mer recurrence; StringDecomposer v1.1.2; TRF v4.10.0rc2; BLAST+; Baum–Welch / forward–backward HMM; binary segmentation; Jensen–Shannon divergence; k-means on canonical k-mer profiles.
