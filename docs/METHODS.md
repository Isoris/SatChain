# SatChain, Methods paragraph (v4.7, 2026-11-01; experimental)

**Satellite DNA (SatChain).** Satellite arrays were annotated with SatChain, a copy-chain framework assembled from established components. Candidate tandem repeats were detected genome-wide without prior annotation. In sliding windows (2 kb, step 1 kb; 10 kb, step 5 kb), the next-recurrence distances of forward-strand 10-mers (lags 2–1,000 bp and 1–5 kb; lags below 7 bp are left to microsatellite callers) were compared with those of a base-shuffled copy of the same window (non-N positions permuted) [NTRprism-like]. Windows exceeding the 99.9th percentile of the null (windows outside known tandem repeats whose best lag is ≥ 7 bp) were merged when their dominant lags agreed (±10 % or 2×/3× multiples); overlapping regions of the two window scales were united and each region's unit was the lag of its best window. A candidate was retained when ≥ 3 consecutive copies were supported (10-mer recurrence at p, p+L and p+2L above a dinucleotide-block shuffle) or, for units of 7–39 bp, when compatible TRF calls [v4.10.0rc2] with ≥ 3 copies covered ≥ 50 % of the candidate; both routes are reported separately. Candidates were decomposed into monomer copies with StringDecomposer [v1.1.2] using iterative class libraries, in which classes were merged unless ≥ 5 near-fixed k-mers (k = 5–20; ≥ 90 % vs ≤ 10 % of copies) separated them; duplicate and overlapping tiles at StringDecomposer's 5-kb batch boundaries were removed before any count. Arrays were then defined on the ordered chain of copies by a two-state hidden Markov model (array vs background), whose emissions were confident (complete, ≥ 80 % identity), weak (65–80 %) and junk (< 65 %) copies and gaps, and whose parameters were learned once per haplotype by Baum–Welch on 400 sampled arrays of the existing and new candidate tables together; chains were broken at assembly gaps and contig joins. Runs were the maximal stretches with posterior ≥ 0.95 holding ≥ 4 confident copies, and each was kept only if its bp-weighted mean k-mer recurrence excess (any lag) exceeded the 90th percentile of windows outside all candidate intervals. Overlapping runs were merged (same family) or trimmed (different families). Tandem gene arrays were separated before any family statistic: a family was a gene array when the annotated gene coverage of its runs reached ≥ 0.7 of that expected for one gene per unit and ≥ 50 % of its units carried an annotated gene (5S rRNA, other rRNA, tRNA, snRNA, miRNA, with barrnap, tRNAscan-SE and Rfam hits merged per class). Family tiers were defined by cumulative share of satellite sequence excluding gene arrays. Within runs, windows of max(1 kb, 20 units), or copy-indexed windows for runs too short for them, carried composition, 4-mer, periodicity, copy-label and local-topology features. Change points were called per feature block by binary segmentation, which at penalties 1.5, 3 and 6 gave nested levels, and were accepted when ≥ 2 independent block groups agreed (composition and 4-mer features counting as one group), at least one of them sequence-only. Runs and segments were summarised as paths of states, namely ranked variants, copies tagged by intrinsic annotations, and annotation and insertion nodes. For each path we derived transition matrices, memory and a structure index against shuffled paths, core and accessory edges, and a second-order test against chains simulated from the path's own first-order matrix with Benjamini–Hochberg control within each reported set. Sequence clusters of satellite monomers (not lineages: no phylogeny is inferred) were obtained by k-means on canonical k-mer profiles (k = 5–7, K = 2–8) of copies sampled from the candidate centromeric runs of both haplotypes, K being chosen as the largest value whose partition was stable across k and across restarts (adjusted Rand index ≥ 0.8) with a silhouette within 10 % of the best, and a family was assigned a cluster only when ≥ 70 % of its sampled copies fell in it; the origin of a family (e.g. 5S rDNA-derived) was stated from annotation overlap and homology, not from the clustering. Families were defined by consensus identity (≥ 80 % over ≥ 50 %). Every threshold named here was swept and is reported as a range in `benchmarks/`. The largest satellite run cluster of a chromosome and the overlap with the earlier TRF-based centromere intervals are reported as candidate centromeric arrays (location only, no functional evidence).

Components: NTRprism-style k-mer recurrence; StringDecomposer v1.1.2; TRF v4.10.0rc2; BLAST+; Baum–Welch / forward–backward HMM; binary segmentation; Jensen–Shannon divergence; k-means on canonical k-mer profiles (randomised truncated SVD, scikit-learn).
