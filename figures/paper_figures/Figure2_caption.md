**Figure 2 | An RTE-clade LINE embedded in the MacSat03 centromeric satellite architecture.** **(A)** Sequence organisation of the 3,117-bp RTE-clade LINE consensus reconstructed from 37 full-length copies. The element contains a single predicted open reading frame encoding 1,027 amino acids, with an N-terminal apurinic/apyrimidinic endonuclease (APE)-like motif and a reverse-transcriptase domain, followed by a 3′ (AAG)n-rich tail. The reverse transcriptase shows its closest match to the RTE clade. Pfam domains (hmmscan of the six-frame open reading frames of the consensus): apurinic/apyrimidinic endonuclease Exo_endo_phos (PF03372, amino acids 95–304, independent *E* = 3.5 × 10⁻¹¹) and reverse transcriptase RVT_1 (PF00078, amino acids 612–846, *E* = 7.6 × 10⁻⁴⁰); no RNase H domain was found, as expected for an RTE-clade LINE. **(B)** Retention of RTE consensus sequence across the MacSat03 centromeric arrays in *Clarias macrocephalus*. Heatmap intensity represents sequence coverage or identity along the RTE consensus, with the number of units indicated for each chromosome. Schematics below distinguish type I composite units, containing a satellite block and flanking RTE-associated sequence, from type II units, containing an additional approximately 6-kb insertion. **(C)** Distribution of RTE core copies within and outside centromeric neighbourhoods in the two subgenomes, stratified by the family group of the centromere: cen, centromeres of the GarSat03 / MacSat03 group (the RTE-carrying centromeric families; SatChain v4.7 sequence cluster C2.1); 5S, centromeres of GarSat04 (the 5S-derived satellite; cluster C2.2); other, centromeres dominated by another family or by a gene array (including the MacSat06 5S rDNA array) and chromosomes without v4.7 runs of record. Stacked bars show the proportion and number of copies within versus outside a ±1-Mb interval around the centromeric region (SatChain v4.7 intervals), with chromosome and copy counts indicated below. The lower plots examine the sensitivity of centromeric enrichment to neighbourhood definitions of ±0.5, ±1 and ±2 Mb. **(D)** Phylogenetic relationships among RTE core copies from both subgenomes. Branch and outer-ring colours distinguish species, position inside a centromere of the GarSat03 / MacSat03 group, of GarSat04 or a 5S rDNA array, or of another family, and non-centromeric copies. A scale bar indicates 0.05 substitutions per site. **(E)** Chromosomal distribution of type II-associated insert sequences in both subgenomes. Grey bars represent chromosomes, with markers distinguishing insert copies of at least 3 kb, shorter fragments of 1–3 kb and centromeric positions. Long insert copies are detected in the *C. macrocephalus* subgenome, whereas no corresponding copies meeting these length thresholds are detected in *C. gariepinus*. **(F)** Detailed organisation of an interrupted MacSat03 array on *C. macrocephalus* LG04 (27.70–28.13 Mb). The upper track shows the distribution of satellite monomer classes, RTE-related fragments and non-satellite interruptions across the array. The enlarged region below illustrates composite units and three spacer classes: A, containing a 245-bp insertion; B, comprising the remaining spacer variants; and C, containing distinct sequence variants. Arrows indicate RTE orientation, and monomer colours distinguish sequence classes. **(G)** Pairwise sequence divergence among RTE copies, comparing copies outside centromeric regions with pooled and individual centromeric arrays in both subgenomes (*C. gariepinus* centromeres split into those of the GarSat03 group and those of GarSat04). Values represent relative sequence divergence rather than calibrated insertion ages; horizontal intervals indicate uncertainty or variation among comparisons. **(H)** Homogenisation of composite units within the MacSat03 centromeric arrays. Observed homogenisation indices (H; 1 indicates complete homogenisation) are shown by chromosome and compared with shuffled-sequence null distributions. Colours distinguish type I and type II centromeres. LG, linkage group; LINE, long interspersed nuclear element; RTE, RTE-clade LINE.
